About The Project:

The MCR Trans Flag is a living artifact that documents how My Chemical Romance has positively impacted their transgender and gender-nonconforming fans. The project spans multiple flags, and stories are continuously submitted and handwritten on the flags. Fan volunteers have helped share these flags with fans at shows around the world, giving trans, gnc folk, and allies the opportunity to sign them in person and continue the tradition of queer visibility and empowerment at My Chemical Romance’s concerts.

How does “MCR [Save] Trans Lives?”

As views on transgender people will become more political and jaded, it becomes harder to find spaces to express oneself, especially for people who are early in their transition or live in an unsupportive environment.

MCR has always been unabashed in its support for outcasts and the marginalized, and made their shows a haven for those ostracized.

So many fans have contributed stories to the flag about how in MCR’s fandom, they’ve found those who have helped them understand themselves and feel seen in a space to gather where they can express themselves in new and affirming ways. Although no one in the band is transgender, the band members' styles have played with gender ambiguity through experimenting with makeup, long hair, and, especially for Gerard, fashion. The band has given outsiders a chance to see people who support them, and they can see themselves in succeeding, creating beautiful, moving art, expressing themselves authentically and unapologetically, and giving those who struggle with the all-too-common experience of suicidal ideation that many trans and gender non-conforming people experience due to societal backlash and lack of acceptance.

The MCR Trans Flag Project began as a way to capture the influence My Chemical Romance has had on the lives of their transgender and gender-nonconforming fans. tradition of queer visibility and acceptance at MyChem shows during a time when trans and GNC people are facing public scrutiny.
